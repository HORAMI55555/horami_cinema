What has this family really inherited?

Synopsis

A documentary crew follows Nim, a medium in northeastern Thailand whose family is believed to inherit a goddess’s presence. When her niece Mink begins behaving strangely, the family wonders whether another succession is beginning. But the changes become increasingly disturbing, drawing the camera into a terrifying intersection of belief and family history.

My thoughts

Complicated, but really interesting. I finally watched this much-talked-about Asian horror. Less frightening than Incantation for me, though probably scary if horror is not your thing. There are so many possible interpretations that I am not sure there is one clear answer. Religious and spirit-based Asian horror has such fascinating cultural texture. The documentary-style presentation still delivers as a horror film. Recommended for fans of that kind of experience!

Cast

Narilya Gulmongkolpech, Sawanee Utoomma

Trivia

This Thai–Korean production brings together Na Hong-jin, involved in the concept and production, and director Banjong Pisanthanakun. The Korean Film Council highlights the collaboration between the filmmakers behind The Wailing and Shutter. One compelling detail is the continued presence of the documentary camera. Explanations offered calmly in interviews become harder to sustain when events unfold. Following that change makes you question whose account to trust—and what may be happening outside the recorded frame.
